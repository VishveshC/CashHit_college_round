## Money, roles, and exits

For a normal ruling, the escrow uses `F = floor(amount * bps / 10000)` and `C = amount - F`. It applies any required losing-side forfeiture to that side's posted deposit, or to that side's own principal payout when no deposit was posted, capped at that payout. The treasury receives only those forfeitures. For every fallback, posted deposits are refunded and no deposit is forfeited. The tested odd-amount, endpoint, missing-deposit, treasury-alias, and multi-milestone cases agree with those rules.

Settlement updates the locked-principal, posted-deposit, claimable, and aggregate-claimable ledgers in the same transaction, without calling the token. Withdrawals debit claimable state before transfer and require exact escrow and recipient balance changes. Disputed deposits and funding use exact incoming balance deltas. The token callbacks exercised by the existing suite cannot reenter guarded mutations. I found no settlement arithmetic that strands a remainder: principal always partitions as `F + C = amount`, including odd raw units.

The immutable owner can only forward arbiter additions and removals. The registry tests cover owner authorization and registry bounds; the money tests and live-dispute invariant cover that owner and handler do not receive a claim. Draw candidates are snapshotted before the first randomness request, retries reuse that snapshot, and selected members keep their vote rights after removal. The pending-draw churn tests cover removal after entropy is available and add/remove churn between failed request attempts. Registry changes before a draw's first request remain within the documented owner registry power.

Panel and seat checks have direct tests: `test_F32_FirstPanelHasThreeDistinctAllowlistedMembersAndExcludesParties` proves the initial panel has three distinct allowlisted addresses excluding both parties; `test_F33_DisplacedNoncommitterCannotCommitOrReveal` proves a replaced no-show loses its current seat; the Panel 2 flow proves it excludes all Panel 1 history. `test_F34_ThreeVotesUseMedianAndTwoVotesUseFlooredMean` covers three-vote and two-vote settlement. Wrong phase and wrong seat are separately distinguished by `test_A31_VoteWrongPhaseIsInvalidStateWhileValidPhaseWrongSeatIsUnauthorized`; that wrong-seat assertion is on Panel 1, while Panel 2 has its own valid-seat deadline coverage.

The stateful handlers are not vacuous in the inspected snapshot. The Phase 3 invariant fixture creates and funds a job, opens a real disputed milestone, and records its deposit. Before the first campaign call, it asserts the live Evidence state and liabilities; its post-invariant check requires a campaign call, exactly one terminalization, a terminal dispute, and backing for all recorded liabilities. Each campaign checks conservation after advances. The existing report correctly limits this to sampled traces rather than exhaustive call-ordering proof. The new 15-advance trace composes two retries in each of the initial, replacement, and second-panel draws with replacement, first-panel no-quorum, and second-panel no-quorum exits.

## Randomness and registry review

The local adapter is a deterministic test provider, not verifiable randomness. Its consumer and request IDs are fixed; callers of its demo `fulfill(requestId)` cannot supply entropy. The engine records pending phase, active request ID, and timeout before the external request; a caught request failure remains retryable. Synchronous callbacks only record the word. Stale, duplicate, unknown, wrong-provider, and late callbacks are handled by the existing tests. Stored timely entropy remains consumable after its request end, while a callback first received at or after the end is ignored.

Each draw snapshots candidates before request attempt zero. Registry churn after that point cannot bias a pending or retried draw, and assigned Panel 1 members retain vote rights after removal. The owner does retain authority over registry contents before the first request for a draw; that is the documented registry power, not an immutable owner ban on pool configuration. The implementation has no production VRF adapter or production pool-minimum enforcement; A-34 records both limitations, and the public-build gate is responsible for rejecting the demo randomness path.
